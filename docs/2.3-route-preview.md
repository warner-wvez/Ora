# Route preview

A way to use Ora the way people use Apple Maps, minus turn-by-turn: find a place, compare the
routes there, and see the cameras on each. It runs on TomTom's APIs, and the page never holds
the key: the live site calls the [relay](1.4-stream-relay.md#tomtom) at relay.wvez.org/tt, and
a page served from this machine calls the [local proxy](../tools/tomtom-eval/README.md). Each
adds the key on its own side. The features turn on only when that server says it has a key;
without one the search box finds cameras and states, and a landmark's card shows its name, kind
and the cameras near it, with no Directions.

The layout copies what people already know. On a computer it follows Google Maps: everything
sits in the left panel. On a phone it follows Apple Maps: a card at the top, the place or the
routes in a sheet at the bottom, and the map in between.

## Recents and Find Nearby

Tap into an empty search box and it opens to two things, the tools two Apple Maps case studies
found people reach for first. In the second, every user ranked the search bar and Recents as the
most important tools on the home screen and 83% wanted Nearby places added; in the first,
people used a map app's own search mostly for the stops on the way: gas, food, coffee, a rest
area.

- **Recents**: the last six places and cameras you picked, newest first, with Clear. They are
  kept in this browser only. Opening a recent place costs no TomTom search; a recent camera turns
  its state on if it is off.
- **Find nearby**: six buttons, Gas, EV charging, Food, Coffee, Rest areas and Parking. One tap is
  one TomTom search for up to eight places within 10 miles, near your blue dot when it is on the
  map, else near the middle of the map when it shows a city or closer, else near your location
  (asked for only then). The places list with their distance and show as pins carrying the
  category's symbol, larger than a camera dot; pointing at a row grows its pin. Pick one from
  the list or the map and its card opens. Back returns to Recents and Find nearby.

## Find a place

With TomTom on, the search box reads "Search a place, address or road". Results list
inside the panel, two lines a row: the name, then the address. A Places group comes from
TomTom after a short pause in typing ("searching…" until it lands). A query that reads like a
road ("I-5", "SR 167", "MP 16", "exit 12") lists Ora's cameras first and only the top three
places after, because TomTom's matches for road text are loose.

Pick a place and the map flies there with a red pin and a card: the name, the address, the
drive time from your location (when the locate button has found you), how many cameras are
within a mile, and one big Directions button that carries the drive time.

## Tap the map

Tap a landmark, a shop or a restaurant drawn on the map and its card opens, the same card a
search pick opens: the name and kind come straight from the map tile, then the address, phone
and website fill in from [Overture Maps Places](https://docs.overturemaps.org/guides/places).
The map keeps your zoom and slides the place clear of the panel or the sheet. Tap empty map
to close the card. Camera pins, state bubbles and route lines answer a tap first, and while
routes are up, taps belong to the routes. On a computer the pointer turns into a hand over
anything a tap would open.

A card borrows Overture's details only for a place with the same name, or nearly the same,
within 150 m. A longer name that adds a facility word ("Parking", "Garage", "Station",
"Entrance") counts as a different place: "Space Needle Observation Deck Parking" must not lend
the deck its category and website. Searched places get the same details the same way.

The details come from `builders/build-places.py`, which reads one region straight from
Overture's files on S3 with DuckDB and writes one small JSON file per zoom-15 map tile into
`places/` (not committed, and read only on this machine until the files are hosted somewhere
public). Washington took 7 seconds: 394,137 places with a confidence of 0.5
or more and not permanently closed, in 29,810 files, 69 MB. In a downtown Seattle sample, 98%
of places had a street address, 80% a website and 78% a phone. Other states show the map
tile's name and kind only until they are built.

    uv run --no-project --with duckdb python builders/build-places.py --bbox W,S,E,N

## Compare routes

Directions starts from your location. The From and To fields are joined by a dotted rail with
a swap button beside them; tap either field to search a new start or destination ("Your
location" is always the first choice for the start). If location is off, the start field opens
for a search.

Avoid, beside Drive at the top, opens two switches: Tolls and Highways. A switch redraws the
routes at once, the chip says what it avoids ("Avoid tolls"), and the choice is remembered in
this browser for the next drive. In the second case study a driver kept paying tolls because
the setting lived outside the app; here it sits where the drive is planned.

Each route lists:

- one label, so the list and the map agree on which is which: Fastest; else Shortest when it
  saves at least half a mile; else Least traffic when it dodges at least two minutes of delay
- a Tolls tag when the route uses a toll road
- the trip time, green when traffic adds little, orange when it adds a quarter, red past that
- the distance, and the roads it is known by ("via I-5 S, WA-167 S")
- "Fastest", or how much slower it is, and the delay from traffic right now
- how many cameras sit within 120 m of it, and how many of those are not working

On the map the chosen route is thick and blue with its slow and jammed stretches coloured
orange and red; the others are grey. Each route has a time bubble pinned to a stretch only it
uses; tap a bubble, a line or a row to choose that route, and the map zooms to fit it, clear of
the panel or the sheet. While routes are up only the chosen route's cameras show, and the state
bubbles step aside.

"Watch these N cameras" saves the route's cameras, in driving order, as one of your routes and
opens it as the scrolling stack of live feeds. Back returns to the place's card; Esc does the
same.

## Preview

Preview tilts the map down to the start of the chosen route and rides the line to the end,
facing the way the road goes and passing the cameras on it, then pulls back to the whole
route. It takes about 0.6 seconds a kilometre, 15 to 45 seconds in all. Touching the map, Esc
or Stop ends it.

## What it does not do

No turn-by-turn, no following your position along the route, and no rerouting. TomTom's terms
call that Navigation Functionality and exclude it. Preview replays the route's line only; it
gives no directions and does not use your position.

## Traffic

Map style gets a Traffic switch: TomTom's flow colours under the map's labels, green to red by
speed against free flow.

## Limits

TomTom's free allowance is counted per month: 2,500 searches (place search and Find nearby
share it), 20,000 routes and 200,000
traffic tiles. The relay stops at 90% of each and the local proxy at 10%, so the two together
stay inside the free month, and the relay gives each address 60 searches, 60 routes and 2,000
tiles an hour. When a limit is hit, place search and routes stop with a short message and the
rest of the map works as before. The counts reset at the start of each month.
